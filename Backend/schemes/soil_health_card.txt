SOIL HEALTH CARD SCHEME

What is it?
The Soil Health Card scheme provides farmers with a free soil testing report, once every 2 years, that shows the nutrient status of their soil (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and other micronutrients) along with specific fertilizer and dosage recommendations tailored to the crops they intend to grow.

Who is eligible?
Any farmer with land under cultivation — owner, tenant, or sharecropper — can apply for a soil health card. There is no income, caste, or land-size restriction for this scheme.

Why does it matter?
- Prevents over-use or under-use of fertilizers, which saves money and protects long-term soil fertility
- Increasingly, having a valid Soil Health Card has become a prerequisite for accessing other input-based subsidies (such as fertilizer or seed subsidies) in several states
- Helps farmers make informed decisions about which crops suit their soil condition

How does it work?
Soil samples are collected from the farmer's field by agriculture department staff or at designated soil testing labs. The sample is tested, and the resulting Soil Health Card is issued to the farmer, either physically or digitally, showing nutrient levels and crop-wise recommendations.

Required documents
- 7/12 utara (land record) or proof of land under cultivation
- Aadhaar card

How to apply
Contact the local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agriculture department office, or apply through the state agriculture DBT portal. Soil sample collection is usually scheduled by field staff after application.

Note: As requirements around mandatory soil testing before other subsidies continue to evolve, confirm the current requirement status with your local agriculture office.
